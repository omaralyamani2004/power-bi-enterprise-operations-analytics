# Business Insights

## Commercial scale and product mix

The unfiltered Commercial Sales Loop view reports `$29.36M` in revenue and `$17.28M` in COGS, producing a `41.15%` gross profit margin. Bikes contribute `$28.32M`, approximately `96.46%` of revenue, with a `40.63%` category margin. Accessories contribute `$700.76K`, but lead order quantity at `36,092` and have the highest category margin at `62.60%`.

The decision insight is therefore about mix, not only volume: Bikes drive scale, while Accessories show stronger margin efficiency. A commercial team could use this distinction to investigate whether accessory bundles, attach-rate improvement, or category-specific pricing can increase profitable revenue without relying only on the dominant Bikes category.

## Regional prioritization

Australia is the strongest region in the current view at approximately `$9.06M`, followed by Southwest at `$5.72M`. Australia represents roughly `30.9%` of total revenue. A commercial team could use this comparison to study whether the gap is driven by product mix, customer coverage, pricing, or fulfillment.

## Customer engagement snapshot

The current Customer Analysis view reports `18,484` customers, `1.50` average orders per customer, `$29.36M` in total sales, and `6,865` returning customers. That is approximately `37.1%` of the customer base. This suggests a practical next step: segment one-time buyers from repeat customers and test retention actions by country, income, education, and marital status.

## Year-over-year interpretation

Using the report's due-date context, 2013 revenue is approximately `$16.04M` versus `$5.84M` in 2012, an increase of about `174.9%`. However, 2014 is only partial through `9 February 2014`, and the shared `DimDate` table does not yet cover the full transaction range. The year-over-year statement should therefore name the comparison period explicitly and should not be presented as a complete-year trend until the date model is repaired.

## Responsible use of the insight

These insights are based on Adventure Works training data and are intended to demonstrate analytical reasoning. They are not claims about a real company's performance. Any customer-level conclusion should be read within its selected date and segment context and checked against the relevant total before being used as a business claim.
